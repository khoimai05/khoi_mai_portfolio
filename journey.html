My Journey
Where it all started
The Beginning
I grew up fascinated by numbers and patterns. Whether it was tracking basketball stats, analyzing game strategies, or just trying to understand why certain teams won and others didn't, I was always drawn to the stories hidden in data. That curiosity eventually led me to Purdue University, where I decided to double down and pursue both Computer Science and Data Science.
Finding My Path at Purdue
Purdue was where things clicked. The coursework gave me a strong foundation in algorithms, statistics, and machine learning, but it was the hands-on projects and internships that really shaped how I think about data. I learned that the best analysis isn't just technically sound -- it tells a story that drives decisions.
Outside the classroom, I immersed myself in the data science community. I attended hackathons, collaborated on research, and spent countless hours building side projects that combined my technical skills with my passion for sports.
Industry Experience
My first real taste of industry came as an Analytics Engineer Intern at OCB, where I built ETL pipelines and dashboards that teams actually relied on. It taught me that data work isn't just about models -- it's about building systems people trust.
At Antsomi, I dove deeper into the data science side as a Data Science Intern. I worked on marketing models and feature engineering, learning how to translate business questions into predictive systems. Every project reinforced the same lesson: the impact comes from understanding the problem, not just the tools.
I also had the opportunity to work as an NLP Researcher, exploring topic modeling and academic text analysis. It was a different flavor of data science -- more research-oriented, more patience required -- but it sharpened my ability to work with unstructured data and think critically about methodology.
Sports and Data
If there's one thread that runs through everything I do, it's sports. I've always believed that sports analytics is one of the most exciting applications of data science -- it combines rigorous statistical thinking with a domain I genuinely love. That belief led me to build NCAA Basketball Analytics (EliteEdge), a project that analyzes a decade of college basketball data. It's the project I'm most proud of because it sits right at the intersection of my skills and my passions.
What's Next
I'm currently looking for data science opportunities where I can keep growing -- ideally at the intersection of analytics, machine learning, and domains I care about. I want to work on problems where data drives real decisions, whether that's in sports, technology, or any field where curiosity and rigor make a difference.
If any of this resonates with you, I'd love to connect.
